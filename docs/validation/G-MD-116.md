# G-MD-116 — Resolve 4W52 Sander finite-difference/GMAX discrepancy

**Finding:** The apparent discrepancy in G-MD-115 came from comparing different energy definitions. Sander's printed EAMBER excludes the positional-restraint term, while the reported minimizer force/GMAX includes the restraint force. The correct total-gradient estimate is the finite-difference derivative of EAMBER **plus** the analytic restraint gradient. For atom 174 (CG, ASP9), this gives an x component of 5.385 kcal mol-1 A-1, consistent with Sander's GMAX 5.3884. No Amber force inconsistency is established by the earlier probe.

## Reconciliation

At the saved checkpoint, central differences of the printed EAMBER at ±0.01 A give the non-restraint gradient estimate:

| Component | d(EAMBER)/dx kcal mol-1 A-1 | Positional-restraint gradient | Estimated total gradient |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| x | +6.140 | −0.755 | +5.385 |
| y | −0.065 | +1.597 | +1.532 |
| z | −1.155 | −0.268 | −1.423 |

The restraint derivative follows the energy convention verified in G-MD-115: E_restraint = 10 × sum(Δx²), so the atomwise derivative is 20 × Δx. The prior G-MD-115 interpretation incorrectly subtracted this gradient and called the EAMBER differences total-potential derivatives. That interpretation has been corrected in G-MD-115; its earlier non-restraint-gradient estimate of 7.148 kcal mol-1 A-1 is withdrawn.

The largest corrected finite-difference component is 5.385 kcal mol-1 A-1. Sander reports GMAX 5.3884 kcal mol-1 A-1 at this atom. A direct force frame generated from the same topology, coordinates, PME/cutoff settings, reference coordinates, restraints and default dispersion setting reports force components (−5.388443, −1.517320, +1.445219) kcal mol-1 A-1, corresponding to a total-gradient x component of +5.388443. Its non-restraint EAMBER is −87552.9642 kcal/mol, matching the zero-cycle calculation. The slight y/z differences from the ±0.01 A finite differences are retained as finite-step/curvature differences; the x component and GMAX agree within the finite-difference resolution.

The matching direct frame was generated with zero initial velocity and exactly one dt=0.000001 ps step solely because the Sander CLI emits its force trajectory through this path. It uses the same default dispersion correction as the minimization energy evaluation. It is a static force-extraction diagnostic, not equilibration, sampling or a production MD run. The saved coordinates differ from the checkpoint by 9.1064e-7 A RMS per Cartesian component (maximum component 3.8142e-6 A). The force frame is corroborative evidence, not a trajectory validation.
